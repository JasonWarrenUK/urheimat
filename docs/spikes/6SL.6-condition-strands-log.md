# 6SL.6 log

Decisions made with Jason while turning the single prosperity number into condition strands (food, numbers, standing), felt through situations, with death and split thresholds. Spike source: 2DS.1 decisions 4g, 5a, 5e and 5f.

## Shape

- The strands form a causal chain. Food feeds numbers each step; numbers is the band's size, and a band whose numbers fail dies. Standing stands apart, moved by storylets and neighbours. Chosen over independent gauges (any strand at zero kills) and over a hidden prosperity aggregate. No scale or set of values for any strand is assumed yet.

## Splits

- Numbers is one avenue to a split, never the only one. Four further avenues are in scope: hunger (food runs short for the band's numbers, so some leave to find land), quarrel (a feud, schism or rival heir ends in a split instead of a fight), open land (a thriving band sends a daughter to claim a rich free tile nearby) and standing (high regard draws followers away under their own leader; low standing drives the shamed out).
- A split normally arrives as a situation, and both accepting and refusing it carry pros and cons. Refusing while the driving pressure remains brings storylets of rising stakes, until a split happens or the pressure eases. A forced split at a threshold is rare: in general it comes from refusing again and again while the pressure mounts. Chosen as a mix of always-a-situation and threshold events.
- Split pressure reuses 6SL.3's pull: each avenue is a pull on the band that builds while its source persists, halves once it has gone and surfaces as a split situation. A refusal sends it back weakened; it rebuilds while the source lasts, and each resurfacing climbs a tier of higher-stakes storylets, the top tier being the forced split. Chosen over a separate split meter.
- Split pulls combine. If an unrelated split pull surfaces while a refused one is still rebuilding, the situation that arises draws on every live split pull at its own tier (for example numbers at tier 2 with hunger at tier 1), never one avenue alone.

## Standing

- Standing is stored per band: each other band's regard for this one, read from its dated knowledge (2k, 2s), so a stale picture gives a stale regard. The aggregate that prestige (4SD.5) needs is derived on read, never stored. The leaders' reputation within their own band is not standing; it waits as a later strand by circumstance and is the natural source of the quarrel avenue. Chosen over an aggregate alone and over modelling both now.
- Four sources move it: storylet answers, size beside the band doing the regarding, being its parent band and shared customs (2v resemblance).
- A storylet answer that moves standing always names the bands whose regard changes. One or several may be named, never none; an answer that moves standing without naming a band is an error. Chosen over spreading every deed to all bands in contact, and over scaling by closeness of contact.
- The standing split avenue as first worded (high regard draws followers away, low standing drives the shamed out) leaned on internal reputation, which standing no longer means. It is deferred to the later internal strand. Chosen over rewording it as being drawn to admirers (too close to absorption, 2t) and over folding regard into open land.
- The strands and split avenues settled so far are not a closed list. More may be added before this task ships.

## Storylet vocabulary

- The `{ prosperity: n }` consequence and condition give way to generic forms keyed by strand id: `{ strand: 'food', by: -1 }` and `{ strand: { id, below, above } }`. A strand that arises by circumstance then needs no new code. Chosen over a named case per strand. The existing answers get a table mapping each to a strand, for Jason's review.

## Death

- Death is diegetic: no `prosperity <= 0` check, but a route through strands, each shown as events in the fiction. A route reads one strand or a combination of strands (the 6SL.5 condition language), and a combination can kill where neither strand alone would.
- The usual route is a ladder, as with splits: lower tiers are situations the player can answer, each answer with pros and cons, and refusing or failing while the strands stay low climbs to a tier whose outcome is the band's end. Sudden one-off storylets that can kill (raid, plague, massacre) are deferred to a later task.
- Four routes are in scope, each a ladder: famine (adequacy stays below need after the buffer is spent), attrition (numbers fall below what the band can keep going, whatever the food), starved and shunned (food with standing) and small and shunned (numbers with standing, ending in absorption (2t) or extinction). Further routes stay later storylets.

## Food and numbers

- Food is relative, not an absolute level: the question is whether there is enough food for the population. Death and split conditions on food must therefore read food against numbers, never food alone.
- Code check: no population exists in the sim today. `prosperityStep` (`engine.ts:399`) moves prosperity by 1.1, minus 5 times the share of food-source values that fight the terrain, plus a flat terrain richness, plus 1.5 per consolidation, minus 1 after a move, clamped to [-2, 2.5]. Today's "food" is what the land and livelihood customs yield, with no reference to mouths, and prosperity mixes yield, headcount and morale.
- Food is a yield (land, terrain richness, fit of the livelihood customs) set against numbers (the headcount). Adequacy is yield against what the numbers need. Chosen over a food stock eaten down and replenished.
- A surplus carries between steps as a buffer, capped by the type of food: how well it can be preserved. A lean step spends the buffer before it becomes famine.
- Yield depends on population and on the nature of the livelihood. All four parts are in scope: a ceiling per source (yield rises with numbers up to it, then flattens, so food per head falls as numbers outgrow it), a labour floor (too few hands and a source yields under its ceiling), overuse depletion (above the ceiling, herds, the hunt and the catch lower their next ceiling for a while) and the labour custom (who works a source changes how many hands count).
- A source interacts with the land: the ceiling is set by terrain fit and richness, so a plough in forest has a far lower ceiling than a plough on open ground. The corpus already carries this as each value's terrain affinities (`strong`, `favours`, `resists`, `excludes`), which the ceiling reads.
- The surplus buffer's cap depends on preservability and on lifestyle together: a granary implies a settled band, so grain stored by a wandering band is capped far lower than the same grain stored by a settled one. Settledness is not built yet (4SD.11 territory, 6SL.15 display), so this task stands in a counter of steps spent on the current seat, with the buffer cap rising as it grows and a move resetting it. Chosen over a fixed multiplier hook and over inferring settledness from the crops held. 4SD.11's note now says to replace the counter with the real settledness custom and revisit the cap curve.
- Preservability lives as a field on each livelihood value in the corpus (for example `keeps: 'high' | 'low' | 'none'`), so a new livelihood value must say how it keeps. Chosen over deriving it from `about` tags, where an edit for another reason would silently change storage.

## Surplus storylets

- In scope: storylets that deal with the notion of surplus at its moments: the band beginning to draw on the buffer, a surplus building, the cap being reached, and so on. Four moments are in scope: surplus building, drawing on the buffer, the buffer nearly spent (where famine's ladder begins) and the cap reached. Loss of the buffer (stores rot, are raided, the herd sickens) is a sudden event and waits for a later task, consistent with the deferral of sudden routes under Death. What each moment offers is open.
- Rationing, an answer under drawing on the buffer, implies a health and/or morale track. That is a candidate new strand arising by circumstance (5e), so strands are not just food, numbers and standing.
- Health and morale are two new, separate strands. Health is the band's physical state: hunger lowers it, good feeding restores it, and it feeds attrition and the famine ladder. Morale is cohesion and willingness to bear hardship: hardship, harsh rationing and quarrels lower it, feasts and good years raise it, and it feeds the quarrel and hunger split avenues. Rationing hurts health and saves stores; a feast lifts morale and spends them. Chosen over either alone and over neither.
- Health and morale always exist, from era 0, with a starting level: they are properties inherent to a band of people. Only their display and the storylets that read them appear by circumstance. This qualifies 5e's "new strands arise according to circumstance": strands that belong to any band of people are always present, and a strand an event brings into being (a later task's) may still be created on first touch. Chosen over creating health and morale on first touch, and over strands that fade.

## Numbers

- Numbers is a hidden headcount with named size tiers over it. Conditions and storylets read the tiers, and the player meets them only as prose, which keeps diegesis (5f) while giving later features concrete numbers (inter-band conflict, daughter band splitting). Food ceilings, labour floors, births and deaths per step and the 'too few to go on' test all work on the headcount. Chosen over a bare headcount and over an abstract 0 to 10 scale. The tier names and boundaries are open.
- A split shares the heads by its reason (4g). Hunger sends out those the land cannot feed, a larger share that leaves the parent with what it can feed. A quarrel sends out a faction, its share set by the quarrel's strength. Open land sends a small party of settlers. The daughter starts with that reason's health and morale: a hunger split is thin, a settler party bold. Chosen over a fixed share and over the player choosing the share.

## Move and consolidate

- A move no longer subtracts 1 from prosperity. It cuts the food buffer (stores do not travel: grain is mostly left behind, herds come with the band) and resets the seat counter that stands in for settledness. The other costs, morale and heads lost on the road, depend on why the band moves: a forced move (hunger, the loser of a quarrel leaving) costs morale and heads, a chosen move (the player's own, or open land) costs little, and a daughter band's settlers pay costs set by the split's reason (11b). This holds for whole-band moves and split daughters alike. The rest of the move costs stay for 4SD.11.
- Consolidate has no special effect. A cautious answer names the strand it improves, through the generic `{ strand: id, by: n }` consequence, and the unused `consolidations` parameter of `prosperityStep` goes. Chosen over a fixed health-and-morale bundle and over a kept consolidate effect.

## Food model

- Yield and need share one unit, heads fed: a source's ceiling is a number of heads, need is the headcount and adequacy is yield over numbers. Chosen over a ration level (rationing and feasting as one lever) and over a bare share of need.
- A source's ceiling is a base figure for the source kind (plough high, gather low; the figures are open) times the terrain affinity weight times one plus the terrain's richness. This reuses the existing affinity and richness tables, so a plough in forest has a fraction of its ceiling on a river. Chosen over affinity alone with richness as a flat bonus and over a new hand-written table.
- A source reaches its ceiling only with enough hands (the labour floor, the hands each kind needs being an open figure) and yields in proportion below that. The working band is a fixed share of numbers (a tuning figure) and splits by the kinds the band recognises. Each kind's workers divide equally among every source that can use that kind: a source worked by one kind draws on that kind, and an all-together source draws on every kind. A source's hands are the sum of what it gets, so fewer workers of any kind mean fewer hands for every shared source, and a kind no source can use sits idle. There is no separate shared pool.
- Labour is divided by the kinds a band recognises, not by an arbitrary two-gender split, and the current labour values (the men, the women, all together) hard-code two. The fix at source is a change to the livelihood custom: each source's worked-by becomes a set of recognised kinds, all-together meaning every kind. It reaches the labour tags that pull descent and residence, the cross-custom constraint on sex nouns (6SL.5), how gender is assigned (4SD.10) and the `unrecognised` condition, so it is its own task, depending on 6SL.6.
- Until that task lands the core's food uses a labelled interim rule: the men and the women each draw on their own kind when the band recognises it, all-together sources draw on every recognised kind, and the other kinds work only all-together sources. With no gender custom the band falls back to men and women. The interim rule is deleted by the labour task.

- One history store: 4SD.15 (record history) depends on 6SL.6 and extends the core's per-step strand snapshots and structured events with custom changes and positions, with its description narrowed to match. Chosen over two stores joined by band and step, and over folding the task into the core.
- Yield against hands is a minimum crew then linear: below a minimum number of hands (a fraction of the hands a source needs, set per kind: a plough needs a team, a herd a herder) a source yields nothing, then yield rises linearly to its ceiling. A tiny band cannot work a plough at all. Chosen over a plain linear curve and a saturating one. The figures for the seven sources are tuning figures; tuning them by evidence script is a proposal Jason has not yet confirmed.
- The resource a source draws on lives on the map tile, not on the band, with a regeneration rate: a band that moves leaves a drawn-down tile to recover, a band that arrives inherits what is there, and a split daughter needs no copying. Chosen over depletion stored on the band.
- Stocks are pools shared by the sources that really share them: soil (hoe and plough crops, one fertility drawn on by different mechanisms), pasture (large and small herds), game (the hunt), fish (the catch) and wild plants (gathering). A tile holds each pool as a fraction of full, stored sparsely for tiles a band has worked and defaulting to full. A source's usable ceiling is its full ceiling times its pool's fraction, harvesting draws the pool down in proportion to the fraction of full ceiling used, and the pool then regenerates.
- Pools are modelled by their ecology, at the richest level: soil regrows only when lightly worked (fallow), the hoe drawing lightly with quick recovery in fallow and a lower ceiling, the plough drawing heavily with a higher ceiling and slower recovery; game and fish breed, regrowth proportional to what remains, so a pool driven near zero recovers very slowly; herds on a tile add to soil regrowth (manure). Jason also asked that the real-world interplay of soil, pasture and fallow land inform these rules, so the rules are checked against sources before they are fixed (open).
- Real-world research (a subagent's report, mostly from search excerpts, with Boserup read only through an EH.net review and several studies as abstracts) supports these rules but fixes no figures: fallow tiers from forest fallow (15 to 20 years, sources vary up to 40) to multicropping, each tier costing more labour per unit of food; herd dung as the main fertility input in mixed systems through folding, convertible husbandry and the three-field system, though sources disagree on how much grazing returns against what it removes; the plough needing draught animals and cutting tillage labour sharply in one Tanzanian study; depensation in cod, common but not universal; and rapid depletion of slow-breeding game (the moa). No verified pasture recovery times or dung-per-hectare figures were found.
- The fallow cycle is not modelled, because a step is 25 years and the cycle (rotation, 1 to 20 year fallows, weeds, the year-to-year choice of how much to clear) happens inside it, with no handle for the player and tuning figures that could not be sourced. What a step can show is the net outcome, so there is one aggregate rule. Load is the fraction of a pool's full ceiling the band used this step. A pool is drawn down in proportion to load times the source's draw (the hoe light, the plough heavy with a higher ceiling), and regrows in proportion to one minus load times the pool's rate, so a lightly used pool recovers, a heavily used one does not and a tile a band has left recovers fully. Fallow is low load, and Boserup's density response emerges by itself because more numbers per ceiling means more load. Game and fish breed (regrowth proportional to what remains), and herds on a tile add to soil regrowth, both at the scale of a step. Chosen over a derived cultivated share and over one rule for every pool.
- The plough needs draught animals: plough crops reach their ceiling only while the band also holds large herds as a source, and otherwise fall to a small hand-hauled ceiling with a higher minimum crew (the figures are tuning figures). Chosen over a link through the pasture pool and over no link. Whether the plough and large herds then pull each other into a band's livelihood, a new row in the cross-custom pull table, is open and not decided.
- Births and deaths are calculated separately, as net rates per 25-year step, and growth or loss is their difference, so a high-birth high-death band differs from a low-birth normal-death band at the same growth. Births read adequacy, health, morale, settledness (the seat counter; the link is unverified) and marriage and descent customs. Deaths read hunger, health, crowding (unverified as a stand-in for density), a violence hook that reads structured events and is zero until 6SL.17 and 4SD.9 exist, and the dangerous lifestyles customs imply. Health still moves toward a target set by adequacy a step at a time, so hunger sickens a band before it thins it.
- Customs reach births and deaths through a table keyed by custom and tag, the same shape as the cross-custom pulls (`when: { custom, about }` gives a birth or death term): a held value about chase or violence adds a death term, a marriage value about a given arrangement a birth term. It reuses the tags the corpus already carries, starts with a few rows and grows, and each row's real-world claim needs a source. Chosen over fields on every value and over an empty list.
- The buffer is a reserve measured as a fraction of a step's need (how many lean years inside a 25-year step the band can absorb), since grain and dried fish cannot be carried from one step to the next. Its cap is the sum over the band's sources of the source's share of the food yield times its keeps factor (high, low or none) times a settled factor that rises with the seat counter from a small floor to one. A surplus beyond the cap is not lost, because it already raised adequacy and so births and health that step. A deficit draws the reserve first, and only what it cannot cover reaches the band as hunger. Chosen over herd-only and over separate herd and granary reserves.
- Tuning figures live in one labelled file, each marked as a tuning figure with the reasoning behind its ratio, and are tuned with an evidence script (in the manner of `pacing.ts`) against targets agreed first (for example: a typical band neither grows without limit nor starves out within a few steps; a plough band on a river beats one in forest; a band that stays fifteen steps on a tile draws its pools down; a move to fresh land recovers). Claude drafts and tunes, and Jason reviews the table and the evidence output before it is committed. Chosen over Jason writing the figures and over deferring tuning to a later task.
## Ladder engine

- A route has two variables. Strength gates surfacing, as in 6SL.3: it builds while the route's feeds hold, fades by half when they stop and surfaces when it reaches a hold. Refusals set the tier: a surfaced situation that is refused sends the pull back at half strength and the next resurfacing is the next tier, and the top tier forces the outcome. Tier steps down one a step once strength has faded.
- An answer's relation to a route is one of three: refuse (the route climbs), ease (the route is bought time or cured at a cost) or resolve (the outcome happens now, by consent, as when a split is accepted). The consequence form is `{ ladder: { route, do: 'refuse' | 'ease' | 'resolve', by? } }`. Letting a situation lie is always a refusal. Every answer to a ladder situation states what it does to each route in play, and a corpus test fails when one is missing, so no effect is implied by absence. Chosen over deriving refusal from the absence of an ease and over a timer.
- Open and not decided here: tier names and boundaries for numbers (absolute headcounts for now, names as placeholders until 2DS.2 designs the display), the wording of the prose lines for strands that stand out, and whether the plough and large herds pull each other into a band.
## Tuning targets

- Target 1 (survival): about three of the six starting bands are still alive after 15 passive steps, on average. Passive runs let every situation lie for every band, so a guided player band should do better. Before tuning, about one in five survived.
- Target 2 (the start): the pre-scattering band already has customs suited to its home tile (`genAncestral` weights each part by affinity squared and never draws a value the home terrain excludes), so the start is built from that and not from a fixed headcount. Total people before the scattering are a share of the home tile's capacity (the ancestral customs on the home terrain, full pools, full crews), divided evenly among the six bands. Each band's adequacy then depends on how well its new tile suits those customs, so the scattering is the first test. Chosen over a share of each band's own capacity and over a depleted home tile.
- The cause of the scattering is out of this task. It belongs to the split ladders (6SL.10, as a first split resolved by consent) or the intro rewrite (6SL.14).
## Open core items

- Plough and herds: the plough and large herds pull each other into a band, as two rows in the cross-custom pull table (a band that holds plough crops is pulled to add large herds in a free livelihood stage), and the draught cliff is gradual: a plough is drawn fully with large herds, partly (0.6 of its ceiling, a crew 1.5 times the minimum) with small herds only and by hand with neither (0.25, twice the crew). Evidence: with customs pinned, a river plough band with large herds does well; unpinned, 6SL.3's land pulls replaced its herds with another plough, which made the plough hand-hauled and killed 24 of 30 such bands. The pull reverses the loss and does not prevent it. Chosen over no row and over rows alone.
- Randomness: births and deaths carry noise that shrinks with size, so small bands swing and can die out by chance. The rule from first principles (Poisson counts of births and deaths, the shape of Lande, Engen and Saether's variance of growth, sigma_e squared plus sigma_d squared over N) gives a growth spread of about the square root of (births plus deaths) over the headcount. It spends the seeded rng, so seeded results and baselines are re-captured. The base figures are to rest on research.
- Research (two subagent reports, mostly search excerpts, with Gurven and Kaplan 2007, Kelly 2013, Boserup 1965, Koyama 1978 and White 2017 read in full; the reports mark what was read and what was not): forager total fertility averages 5.7 (range under 4 to about 8) and life expectancy at birth runs 21 to 37, so the births base of 1.1 and deaths base of 0.8 a step are consistent; forager growth averages about 1% a year with a long-run saw-tooth near zero; populations under 40 are not viable over 400 years and about 150 is reliably so (White 2017); 2,000 kcal a head a day; about 25% of foragers hunt and the !Kung gathered about 1.2 times their need; per-worker productivity is about 2.4 heads fed for foragers and 1.3 to 2.8 for English farm workers, so about 2 to 3 for every source, where the model's hands needed implied 2 to 10; densities per km2 are foragers 0.1 to 0.4 (0.5 to 3 on rich land), Northwest Coast fishers 0.1 to 2, Jomon fishers 0.89 average and 3 best, forest-fallow hoe farming 5 to 10, intensive hoe farming up to 120. No source gave deaths per share of food missing (famine totals run 3.5% to 12% of a population an episode, and the hunger coefficient of 1.5 is an assumption), pool draw or regrowth rates, minimum crews, herd productivity per worker or pasture recovery times, and these remain assumptions.
- The model follows the density research in full: farming supports 10 to 30 times the people per km2 that foraging does. Densities are per km2, so a tile has an area, held as one labelled figure. The corollary, set by Jason, is that the single-seat-tile model gives way to a tiles-needed model: a band works as many tiles as its numbers require. This reverses part of spike decision 3k (a single seat sets fit and strain, claimed tiles only affect contact, ownership and crowding) and widens 4SD.11. How it is built is open.
## Range: the tiles a band needs

- Range is built in the core now, scoped to food and livelihood needs. Two follow-on tasks come after the core: a much bigger map first (the density figures mean a band can need many tiles, so the 9 by 7 map is too small), and then a wider tile-range task that explores how range interacts with the other mechanisms (territory 4SD.11, daughter bands, moves, conflict, standing, and the sharing of tiles). That task includes an explicit design spike on how bands share tiles. Chosen over folding range into 4SD.11 and over a separate task after the core.
- Tiles are a shared commons for now: any tile can be worked by several bands except another live band's seat, pools are drawn by the sum of everyone's load, nothing is claimed against other bands, and crowding shows as worn pools. Chosen over exclusive first-come claims and over persistent claims (territory).
- A source carries a density per km2, a per-hand productivity (heads fed per worker, about 2 to 3) and a minimum team size in absolute hands, in place of a ceiling, hands needed and a crew fraction. It yields the lesser of what its tiles' ceilings allow and what its hands produce, and nothing below its minimum team. A tile has an area (a labelled figure, nominally 100 km2). Tiles needed follow from what the band can work: the smaller of its numbers and its hands times productivity.
- Range behaves two ways. Itinerant sources (the hunt, gathering, the catch and herds) pick their tiles afresh each step: the seat plus the nearest tiles by distance, the richest first among ties, until their capacity covers what the band can work, within a reach of 2, never water or another live band's seat. Infrastructure sources (hoe crops and plough crops) work fields, tiles the band holds across steps and stores: fields are added nearest first within reach 1 only when the band needs more than its fields give, are kept while worked, and are given up only after they have gone unneeded for several steps in a row (a labelled figure, 3). The choice follows Jason's point that recalculating each turn suits foraging and not farming.
